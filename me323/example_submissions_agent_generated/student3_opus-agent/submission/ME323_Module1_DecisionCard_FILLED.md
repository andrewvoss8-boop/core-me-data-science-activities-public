Beams 16 and 17 sit at `Mcr/My ≈ 0.99` with zero or negative separation margin and miss by 22%; beam 14 has negative separation margin but `Mcr/My = 1.43` and misses by 7%; the eleven bend-governed thick-web beams have ratios of 0.90 to 1.09 with no pattern left over. The GP cannot see that structure because it interpolates the ratio field from five thin-web points that are all in the marginal regime; it therefore carries their 20%+ knockdown into a region where we argue it does not apply. **We do not average the two numbers.** We predict the physics is closer here and we have staked the print on it. |

| 10 | **Falsifier.** | Two, and they point in different directions. **(a)** A measured strength at or below **555 N (33.5 N/g)**, the lower 95% bound: that would put the miss outside the model's own interval and mean the physics bias is broader than mode boundaries, killing lane D's physics prior in this half of the box. **(b)** Any recorded flange–web separation, at any load: that falsifies the margin argument directly, since the calibrated model gives separation 42% of headroom here and would need `τ_i` below 11.8 MPa to be wrong. | The one assumption most likely to break this prediction | That the 20%+ physics overprediction seen at beams 15, 16, and 17 is caused by the governing branch running out of margin, and therefore does not apply where both margins are large. | The outcome that would change our next design | A recorded flange–web separation at any load, or a measured strength below 555 N. Either sends us back to `b ≥ 2.2` where the separation margin exceeds 100%, and it retires lane D in favour of a plain GP for the rest of the module. A result above 700 N sends the class the other way, into `b ∈ [1.6, 2.2]` at `H_web` 12 to 13. |
