# ME 323 Module 1 — Design Decision Card

One page per design gate, same ten questions every time. Your group fills this
card twice: once with Submission 1 (before your beam is printed — rows 1–9 plus
the preregistration table) and once with Submission 2 (add row 10). The card is
the skeleton of your memo: every memo prompt maps onto a row, and graders read
the card first. Short answers are fine; each row should be one to three
sentences, with numbers where numbers exist.

The card exists to separate reasoning from storytelling. After the test,
everyone can sound insightful. The card is your group on record *before* the
test: what you predicted, what you feared, and what would have changed your
mind.

## The card

| # | Row | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Decision.** What beam do you recommend? `(b, H_web)` and its estimated mass. | |
| 2 | **Physics.** What capacity and governing failure mode does the calibrated mechanics model predict there? Include the runner-up mode and its margin. | |
| 3 | **Data.** What does your GP predict there (posterior median, in the units you model)? | |
| 4 | **Uncertainty.** Epistemic sigma at your design, and the total predictive interval for one future test. | |
| 5 | **Support.** How close is this design to tested beams, and what did the nearest ones do (strength *and* failure note)? | |
| 6 | **Disagreement.** Where do the physics and the GP disagree near your design, and what is your best explanation of why? | |
| 7 | **Risk posture.** Is this pick exploiting, exploring, or replicating? Name your acquisition rule and dial value. | |
| 8 | **Alternative.** What credible design did you reject, and what tipped the choice? | |
| 9 | **Falsifier.** What test outcome — a load, a failure morphology, or both — would make you abandon this model or this region? | |
| 10 | **Update** *(after the test)*. What did the result change: your model, your confidence, or your next design? Be specific about which assumption moved. | |

## Preregistration (filed with Submission 1, before printing)

Committed before print day. Graded on discipline, not on being right: a sharp
prediction that misses beats a vague one that cannot miss. "About 500 N" with
no interval, or "it might separate or bend," earns nothing here.

| Quantity | Your prediction |
|---|---|

| Calibrated-physics capacity (N) and governing mode | |

## How this is graded

The card feeds the rubric's synthesis, preregistration, and interpretation
criteria (see `ME323_Module1_Rubric.md`). Three questions decide whether the
card is strong, and a grader will ask them in order:

1. Why this beam instead of the alternative in row 8?
2. What evidence would have changed your choice *before* printing (row 9)?
3. After testing, what changed beyond the dataset gaining one row (row 10)?

A card that cannot answer those is weak no matter how polished the memo built
on it. Note what rows 2–6 require: you must run both models *at your own
candidate* and put their numbers side by side. If they agree, say why you
believe the agreement (nearby data? shared assumptions?). If they disagree,
row 6 is where the module's whole argument lives — do not average the two
numbers and move on.
